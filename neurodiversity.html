Neurodiversity

TƎT and neurodiversity: language for capability, not diagnosis

You do not need a diagnosis to use TƎT. If you have one, it still fits.

This page explains the words, where TƎT sits next to them, and what it leaves alone.

The words

Neurodiversity is the idea that human minds vary naturally, and that this variation is part of being human, not a defect to be fixed. The concept grew out of autistic activism in the 1990s and was built collectively, not by one person (Botha et al., 2024).

Neurodivergent describes a mind that differs from what is typical. The activist Kassiane Asasumasu is credited with the word. In everyday use it now covers autism, ADHD, dyslexia and more, with or without a formal diagnosis.

Where TƎT sits

TƎT names eight cognitive talents: integrative thinking, creative implementation, strategic empathy, cognitive dexterity, embodiment, deep focus, emotional intelligence and regulation, and adaptive masking. Each starts as friction, a behaviour that is useful and still looks excessive or misplaced.

Nothing is scored. There is no dominant talent and no correct constellation. TƎT also does not claim that every difficulty is secretly a gift.

People arrive from different places: with a diagnosis, without one, unsure, or because they work with someone who thinks differently. They all get the same eight stories.

Why there are no diagnostic words

A label answers the wrong question

A diagnosis says what is wrong. TƎT asks what this environment needs, and when. The Antwerp Maritime Academy case is built on that second question.

The line is blurry in practice

In a 2025 Understood survey of more than 300 people working in advertising, marketing, PR and media, 48% identified as neurodivergent, counting formal diagnoses, self-diagnoses and self-perception. The report sets that against 31% for the general population, without saying where that number comes from (Understood, 2025). A tool that waits for a diagnosis misses a lot of people who recognise the pattern.

A shared vocabulary moves the conversation

A team that can name what a colleague contributes can stop arguing about who that colleague is. In the maritime case, students began saying things like “this needs someone to hold the question open.” The disagreement moved from the person to the task.

What TƎT leaves alone

  • Diagnosis and assessment. TƎT does not screen, test or label.
  • Support and adjustments. If someone needs them, they need them whichever stories they recognise.
  • Selection. TƎT is not used to select, rate, rank or assess individuals.
